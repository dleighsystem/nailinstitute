# Neuravant AI Evidence Pilot Proposal for Hiscox / Lloyd’s Market Participants

## Purpose

AI adoption in the Lloyd’s market is accelerating, but insurers still need defensible governance, human oversight, control validation, and claims-grade evidence. Neuravant proposes a short evidence pilot to show how AI-agent activity can be monitored, risk-scored, contained, and converted into underwriter-readable records.

## Pilot Proposition

Neuravant provides **runtime assurance and evidence-chain reporting for AI-enabled insurance workflows**. The pilot will demonstrate how an insurer can answer four practical questions:

1. What did the AI agent or AI-assisted workflow actually do?
2. Was a human required, notified, overridden, or bypassed?
3. Which governance control applied, and did it work?
4. Is there a tamper-evident evidence record suitable for underwriting, audit, or claims review?

## Recommended Pilot Scope

**Duration:** 30-60 days  
**Participants:** Underwriting, risk, actuarial, claims, security, and innovation stakeholders  
**Environment:** Simulated or controlled AI-agent workflow; no production data required for initial pilot  
**Focus:** Runtime telemetry, anomaly scoring, containment decisions, human accountability, and evidence reporting

## Demonstration Scenarios

| Scenario | What It Shows | Underwriting Relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal AI-assisted workflow | Baseline safe behaviour | Establishes expected control state |
| Retry-loop / runaway process | Agent instability or operational failure | Supports operational risk assessment |
| Tool bypass attempt | AI action outside approved policy | Tests governance and control enforcement |
| Privilege or sensitive-access probe | Escalation or unauthorised access risk | Supports security and vendor-risk review |
| Human override / approval | Human-in-the-loop accountability | Supports causation and claims defensibility |

## Pilot Deliverables

- **AI-agent risk taxonomy:** A practical classification of runtime behaviours relevant to underwriting and governance.
- **Runtime telemetry schema:** A record format for tool calls, process activity, sensitive access, policy decisions, and containment actions.
- **Underwriter evidence pack:** A concise report showing risk tier, control response, human oversight, and incident timeline.
- **Governance mapping table:** A mapping from observed behaviour to AI governance controls and oversight obligations.
- **Claims attribution scenario:** A worked example distinguishing AI action, human action, vendor/tool failure, and control failure.
- **Tamper-evident evidence chain:** A signed/hash-linked export proving evidence integrity across the pilot record.

## Success Criteria

The pilot is successful if Hiscox or a Lloyd’s market participant can use the output to:

- Assess whether an AI-enabled workflow is operating inside declared controls.
- Distinguish autonomous agent behaviour from human-approved or human-overridden action.
- Identify which control fired, failed, or was bypassed.
- Produce a defensible evidence record for audit, governance, underwriting, or claims review.
- Translate AI operational behaviour into underwriting questions, warranties, exclusions, or pricing considerations.

## Meeting Ask

Neuravant is seeking a bounded pilot conversation with Hiscox or Lloyd’s market participants to validate the evidence model, underwriting usefulness, and governance reporting format.

Suggested ask:

> Would Hiscox be open to a 30-60 day controlled evidence pilot focused on AI-agent runtime risk, human oversight, and underwriter-readable governance reporting?

## Why Now

The Lloyd’s Market Association has highlighted rapid growth in AI adoption and governance activity across the market. The next challenge is not whether insurers will use AI, but whether they can evidence that AI-enabled processes are controlled, accountable, and insurable.

Neuravant’s opportunity is to become the evidence infrastructure layer for that transition.

## Positioning Line

> Neuravant helps insurers adopt AI safely by making AI-agent behaviour observable, governable, attributable, and defensible.
