# Neuravant Insurable Autonomy One-Pager

## Positioning Line

Neuravant helps make autonomous AI workflows insurable by enforcing controls before consequential actions, preserving signed evidence after key decisions, and translating runtime behaviour into underwriting-grade risk signals.

## The Problem

Insurance firms are moving from AI-assisted analysis to agentic workflows that can read submissions, call tools, update systems, recommend pricing, draft communications, and trigger operational decisions. That changes the risk profile.

A poor chatbot answer is a quality issue. An autonomous agent issuing a flawed quote, changing reserves, mishandling claims, bypassing policy, or writing to a production database can become a regulated loss event.

The core barrier is not only hallucination. It is **unaccountable autonomy**: consequential actions without reliable evidence of intent, control, approval, causation, or policy compliance.

## The Neuravant Answer: Insurable Autonomy

Neuravant provides an evidence-grade governance layer for autonomous AI workflows. It helps insurers, brokers, managing agents, reinsurers, auditors, and boards answer five practical questions:

1. What did the agent attempt to do?
2. Was the action allowed, blocked, held, or escalated?
3. Which policy or control made that decision?
4. Who approved, rejected, modified, or overrode it?
5. Can the record support audit, claims review, and underwriting decisions?

## How It Works

| Control Layer | What It Does | Insurance Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe | Captures agent trajectory, tool calls, state transitions, retries, and drift indicators | Shows what actually happened inside the workflow |
| Interrupt | Holds consequential actions for human approval before execution | Preserves human accountability and governance sign-off |
| Constrain | Routes high-risk actions through schemas, policy gates, and deterministic compute engines | Reduces uncontrolled tool use and hallucinated financial outputs |
| Challenge | Uses checker agents and compliance supervisors to review assumptions, citations, calculations, and policy fit | Creates real-time digital peer review before exposure |
| Attest | Produces signed, hash-linked decision records for consequential actions | Creates claims-grade evidence of control, causation, and integrity |

## Example: Actuarial Workflow

An AI agent reviews a broker submission, extracts exposure data, maps policy clauses, proposes assumptions, and prepares pricing inputs. Before final quote issuance, Neuravant holds the action.

The chief actuary sees:

- Source documents and extracted fields
- Proposed model parameters
- Deterministic calculation output
- Checker-agent objections
- Applicable governance policy
- Required approval reason
- Signature-chain status

The actuary can approve, reject, modify, or escalate. That decision is recorded as part of the signed evidence chain.

## Underwriting and Claims Value

Neuravant converts runtime behaviour into evidence carriers can use.

| Runtime Evidence | Underwriting / Claims Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Clean signed chain | Stronger audit and claims defensibility |
| Missing or broken chain | Coverage concern or exclusion trigger |
| Frequent hold events | Workflow needs tighter controls or training |
| High-risk human overrides | Governance weakness or moral hazard indicator |
| Tool-bypass attempts | Elevated operational and security risk |
| Drift or retry loops | Instability risk and possible premium load |
| Checker-agent disagreement | Model uncertainty or process-risk signal |
| Deterministic computation path | Reduced exposure to hallucinated financial figures |

This allows insurers to distinguish autonomous error, human-approved action, vendor/tool failure, control failure, external compromise, and excluded conduct.

## Pilot Proposition

Neuravant proposes a bounded 30-60 day evidence pilot for a controlled AI-enabled insurance workflow.

Pilot outputs:

- AI-agent risk taxonomy
- Runtime evidence schema
- Human oversight and override record
- Governance mapping table
- Claims attribution scenario
- Signed evidence-chain export
- Underwriter-readable evidence pack

## Success Criteria

The pilot succeeds if a Lloyd’s market participant, carrier, or reinsurer can use the output to:

- Assess whether an AI-enabled workflow operated inside declared controls.
- Identify which policy fired, failed, or was bypassed.
- Distinguish autonomous action from human-approved action.
- Produce a defensible record for audit, underwriting, or claims review.
- Translate AI operational behaviour into warranties, exclusions, pricing, or remediation requirements.

## Board-Level Summary

Neuravant does not ask boards to trust AI. It gives them a control plane that proves what the AI attempted, what was prevented, who approved consequential actions, and whether the resulting risk is insurable.
